Every business meets a difficult customer sooner or later. What matters is what happens next.

On Sunny 88.7 FM, client relations specialist Emma Clara Goode explained how a tense moment at the counter or on the phone can become the start of a lasting relationship.

She set aside the old saying that "the customer is always right." Her approach is simpler, and harder: stay factual, stay calm and focus on the solution.

![In the Sunny FM studio](/assets/uploads/emma-clara-goode-customer-loyalty-studio.jpg)

### Keep Your Cool

An upset customer is often reacting to how they feel they are being treated, not only to the problem itself. A calm, respectful tone is the first step in cooling things down.

### Listen Fully

Let the customer finish. Acknowledge their frustration and show you understand before you explain anything.

### Stick to the Facts

Don't argue. Check the details. If your business made a mistake, own it without excuses. The customer does not need to hear which department dropped the ball.

### Offer a Clear Way Forward

Customers want results, not a tour of your internal processes. Once you know what went wrong, tell them exactly what you will do about it.

### Make Good Service a Daily Habit

Excellent service is not a theory, she stressed. Regular training, team huddles and role-play help front-line staff handle problems before they grow.

### Why It Pays

When a business takes responsibility and offers a real solution, trust grows. Even where a problem cannot be fixed perfectly, meeting the customer halfway can turn a bad moment into loyalty.

Treat every customer as equally important, and good word of mouth follows.

As Proverbs 15:1 puts it, "A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger."
