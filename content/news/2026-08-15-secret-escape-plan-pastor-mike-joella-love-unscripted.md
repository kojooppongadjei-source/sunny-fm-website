The ticket was always there.

Tucked away but never forgotten, it was renewed year after year for more than a decade. It wasn't booked for a holiday or a family visit. It was something far more personal.

It was an exit strategy.

Whenever life became unbearable, Pastor Joella knew she could leave Ghana and return to the United Kingdom. She never imagined that one day, letting that ticket expire would become one of the most important decisions she would make in her marriage.

Sharing the story publicly for the first time on **The Couple Next Door**, Sunny 88.7 FM's Love Unscripted series on the **Inspirational Morning Drive (IMDrive)**, Pastor Joella did not try to present herself as the perfect Christian wife. Instead, she offered something far more powerful. She offered honesty.

![](/assets/uploads/pastor-joella-love-unscripted-interview.jpeg)

### A Little Girl Who Decided Marriage Wasn't for Her

What makes her story remarkable is that it did not begin with marriage. It began with a little girl sitting quietly in her father's church.

As a pastor's daughter, Joella often watched women arrive seeking counselling. They came carrying stories that no child should have to process. Some spoke through tears. Others bore the weight of emotional wounds, financial abuse and broken trust. Although no one intended for her to hear those conversations, she did.

Without realising it, she began writing her own story.

By the age of nine, she had already reached a conclusion. Marriage was not for her.

### Meeting Pastor Mike

Years later she met Pastor Mike.

He was kind, patient and deeply committed to Christ. Everything about him contradicted the picture of marriage she had built in her mind. She loved him enough to marry him, but fear has a way of surviving even the happiest wedding day.

The vows changed her surname. They did not immediately change her heart.

"I came into marriage with guards and shields," she admitted. "Make the slightest mistake and you would feel my wrath."

For Pastor Mike, those early years were confusing. Before they married, he believed God had spoken a simple promise to him: "She will make you a good wife."

There were moments when that promise seemed impossible to reconcile with reality. Arguments came. Misunderstandings followed. Yet he chose a response that Joella never expected.

He refused to fight her. Instead, he prayed. He served. He listened.

After difficult days she would come home to discover that he had prepared a meal, made her tea or quietly taken care of household chores. They were ordinary acts, almost invisible to everyone else. But over time they became impossible for her to ignore.

"I was always looking for a fight," she recalled. "He would never fight me back."

### Leave. Return. Carry On. Repeat.

Still, something inside her remained unconvinced.

Whenever conflict became overwhelming, she would board a flight to the UK. The distance helped them breathe again, but it never healed what had caused the pain. By the time she returned, life had settled, emotions had cooled and the difficult conversations had been postponed once again.

It became a cycle. Leave. Return. Carry on. Repeat.

### The Prayer That Changed Everything

Joella says she went before God expecting Him to reveal what her husband needed to fix.

Instead, she felt God turning the spotlight on her.

She realised the ticket had become more than a travel document. It had become permission to avoid the uncomfortable work that every lasting marriage eventually demands. As long as an escape remained within reach, complete commitment remained just out of reach.

She sensed God asking her to trust Him enough to stay. Not for another week. Not until the next argument. Stay.

When renewal time arrived, she watched the expiry date draw closer. For thirteen years she had always renewed the ticket before it expired.

This time she did nothing.

She remembers watching the days disappear from the calendar until the ticket finally became worthless.

"It had to burn," she said.

The paper expired quietly. The fear it represented did not disappear overnight. But for the first time, she had removed her escape route and committed herself fully to building the marriage she once believed was impossible.

### Nineteen Years, Six Sons and a Home Still Being Built

Today, Pastor Mike and Pastor Joella have been married for nineteen years and are raising six sons together. Their home, they admit, is still far from perfect. Their children tease them for being overly affectionate and laugh at how openly they express love for one another.

They laugh too. Because they know those boys are learning something no sermon alone could ever teach.

They are watching a husband honour his wife. They are watching a wife who once feared marriage discover joy within it. They are watching forgiveness become a habit instead of an event.

There was a moment during the interview when the host asked what had carried them through the hardest seasons.

Neither of them spoke about having all the answers. Neither claimed they had discovered a secret formula.

Instead, they spoke about choosing to remain at the table when walking away seemed easier. About having uncomfortable conversations. About allowing God to deal with the wounds each of them had carried into the relationship.

Perhaps that is why their story resonates. It is not the story of a flawless marriage. It is the story of two people who discovered that healing often begins long before circumstances change. Sometimes it begins the moment someone decides to stop preparing for the exit and starts believing that, with God's help, there is still a future worth fighting for.
